CNC Spinning of Kettle Bodies

A CNC machine built around a RichAuto controller, with step-by-step forming toolpaths designed in SolidCAM, to automate kettle-body forming that used to be done by hand.

Year 2019 – 2020Metal spinningAutomationSolidCAMCNC
Kettle bodies formed in the workshop

A kettle factory in Alborz province had a bottleneck in forming kettle bodies: they are made by spinning 0.7 mm sheet metal over a mandrel — work done by hand that depended on the operator's skill. To automate it, I built a CNC machine around a RichAuto controller, including its wiring and electrical cabinet. I then designed and simulated the roller's path step by step in SolidWorks and SolidCAM, and each step ran as a separate G-code program on that machine, so that if one step went wrong the work could resume from that step. The target shape came from a 3D scan of the reference sample.

Workshop tests showed that part quality depends above all on the tailstock force behind the kettle and on seating the seven-piece mandrel correctly; a shortfall in either caused vibration and tore the rim. To compensate for the sheet's springback, the tool runs slightly past the scanned profile and lifts 5 mm after leaving the part so it does not hit the rim as it springs back. The toolpath curves were kept smooth, with no sharp breaks.

My role

Built the CNC machine with a RichAuto controller, including the wiring and electrical cabinet; designed and simulated the step-by-step forming toolpaths in SolidWorks and SolidCAM, prepared the G-code programs, and tested and refined the paths on the machine at the factory.
